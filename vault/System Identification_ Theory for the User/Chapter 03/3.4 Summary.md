# 3.4 Summary

> [!tip] Pre-Reading Primer
> **Role in Chapter 3**: Section 3.4 distills the entire theoretical arc of Chapters 2 and 3 into a single operational takeaway: whether derived via stochastic disturbance modeling or deterministic observer design, the practical output of a linear system description is the linear predictor filter of Equation (3.46), setting the stage for the parameterized model structures of Chapter 4.

---

## 1. Core Intuition & Engineering Motivation

- **Stochastic Assumptions as Vehicles for Predictor Design**: A central philosophical message of Lennart Ljung's framework is that the underlying probabilistic assumptions about the disturbance $v(t) = H(q)e(t)$ are not ends in themselves. In practical engineering applications—such as forecasting, model validation, or minimum-variance control—what actually matters is **how predictions are computed** from observed input-output data. The stochastic noise model $H(q)$ and the observer framework of Section 3.3 are ultimately *vehicles* ("a methodology for guessing future system outputs") that guide the engineer to a concrete, stable linear predictor filter.
- **Unifying Stochastic Prediction and Deterministic Observers**: Section 3.4 reinforces that the one-step-ahead predictor $\hat{y}(t|t-1)$ and the $k$ -step-ahead predictor $\hat{y}(t|t-k)$ do not strictly require belief in a true physical white-noise source $e(t)$ . One arrives at the exact same two-input linear filter structure through purely deterministic state-observer considerations (Section 3.3) by choosing how rapidly historical initial-state mismatches and unmeasured disturbances should decay.
- **Bridge to Chapter 4 (Parameterized Model Structures)**: While Equations (3.46) and (3.31) express the predictors in terms of general infinite impulse response (transfer-operator) filters $G(q)$ and $H(q)$, inverting and truncating general transfer functions directly via power-series expansions can be computationally cumbersome. When $G(q)$ and $H(q)$ are endowed with specific rational structures—such as ARX, ARMAX, Output-Error (OE), or Box-Jenkins (BJ) models, or state-space representations—computing $\hat{y}(t|t-1)$ reduces to efficient, finite-order difference equations. This structural specialization is the primary focus of Chapter 4.
- **Historical & Bibliographic Context (Section 3.5)**: As Ljung notes in the accompanying bibliographic remarks, once a disturbance process is represented in the canonical innovations form $v(t) = H(q)e(t)$ via spectral factorization (Kolmogorov, 1941; Wiener, 1949; Whittle, 1963) or its time-varying state-space counterpart via the Riccati equation (Kalman, 1960; Kalman and Bucy, 1961), the algebraic calculation of the predictor itself becomes straightforward. However, because spectral factorization only captures second-order properties (spectrum/covariance), the linear representation $v(t) = H(q)e(t)$ is fundamentally tailored to Gaussian (or wide-sense stationary second-order) descriptions.

## 2. Mathematical Rigor & Derivations

### 2.1 Symbol & Variable Glossary

| Symbol | Meaning / Physical Interpretation | Space / Dimensions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $t$ | Discrete-time index (assuming unit sampling interval $T = 1$ ) | $\mathbb{Z}$ |
| $q$ , $q^{-1}$ | Forward and backward shift operators: $q^{-1}u(t) = u(t-1)$ | Operator on $\ell^\infty$ |
| $u(t)$ | Measured deterministic input signal at time $t$ | $\mathbb{R}$ |
| $y(t)$ | Measured system output signal at time $t$ | $\mathbb{R}$ |
| $e(t)$ | Sequence of independent and identically distributed (white noise) random variables with zero mean and variance $\lambda$ | $\mathbb{R}$ (stochastic) |
| $G(q)$ | Deterministic input-to-output transfer operator, $G(q) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} g(k)q^{-k}$ | Rational / stable LTI filter |
| $H(q)$ | Monic, causal, and inversely stable noise transfer operator, $H(q) = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} h(k)q^{-k}$ | Monic min-phase LTI filter |
| $H^{-1}(q)$ | Causal and stable inverse noise filter mapping $v(t)$ back to the innovation $e(t)$ | Stable LTI filter |
| $\bar{H}_k(q)$ | Truncated causal polynomial of the first $k$ impulse response coefficients of $H(q)$ , $\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1} h(\ell)q^{-\ell}$ | FIR polynomial of order $k-1$ |
| $W_k(q)$ | $k$ -step-ahead predictor weighting filter, defined as $W_k(q) = \bar{H}_k(q)H^{-1}(q)$ | Stable LTI filter |
| $\hat{y}(t|t-1)$ | One-step-ahead prediction: best "guess" of $y(t)$ given $u(s)$ and $y(s)$ for $s \le t-1$ | $\mathbb{R}$ |
| $\hat{y}(t|t-k)$ | $k$ -step-ahead prediction: best "guess" of $y(t)$ given $u(s)$ for $s \le t$ and $y(s)$ for $s \le t-k$ | $\mathbb{R}$ |
| $\hat{y}(t|\infty)$ | Pure simulation output: output prediction using only input history $u(s)$ for $s \le t$ (no output measurements) | $\mathbb{R}$ |

### 2.2 Formal Definitions, Assumptions & Step-by-Step Derivations

#### 1. The Canonical Linear System Representation
Chapter 3 starts from the standard additive linear time-invariant (LTI) system representation established in Chapter 2:

$$
y(t) = G(q)u(t) + H(q)e(t)
$$

To guarantee a well-posed prediction problem, $G(q)$ and $H(q)$ satisfy:
1. **Causality and Stability**: Both $G(q)$ and $H(q)$ are causal and exponentially stable filters.
2. **Monicity of** $H(q)$ : $H(q) = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} h(k)q^{-k}$ starts with a leading $1$ (so no instantaneous feedthrough of $e(t)$ is scaled or lost, and $1 - H^{-1}(q)$ contains strictly delayed terms $q^{-1}, q^{-2}, \dots$ ).
3. **Invertibility (Minimum-Phase Condition)**: The inverse filter $H^{-1}(q)$ is also causal and exponentially stable, ensuring that past innovations $e(s)$ for $s \le t-1$ can be stably reconstructed from past inputs and outputs.

#### 2. The Fundamental One-Step-Ahead Predictor (Equation 3.46)
Given past inputs and outputs $u(s)$ and $y(s)$ for $s \le t-1$ (plus any known current input $u(t)$ if $G(q)$ were non-strictly proper, though typically $G(q)$ has at least a one-sample delay), the optimal one-step-ahead prediction $\hat{y}(t|t-1)$ is obtained by substituting $e(t) = H^{-1}(q)\left[y(t) - G(q)u(t)\right]$ into $\hat{y}(t|t-1) = y(t) - e(t)$ :

$$
\hat{y}(t|t-1) = H^{-1}(q)G(q)u(t) + \left[1 - H^{-1}(q)\right]y(t) \tag{3.46}
$$

- **Causal Dependence Check**: Because $H(q)$ is monic, its inverse $H^{-1}(q) = 1 + \tilde{h}(1)q^{-1} + \tilde{h}(2)q^{-2} + \cdots$ is also monic. Therefore, the output filter $1 - H^{-1}(q) = -\tilde{h}(1)q^{-1} - \tilde{h}(2)q^{-2} - \cdots$ contains at least a one-step delay $q^{-1}$ , proving that $\hat{y}(t|t-1)$ depends strictly on past output measurements $y(s)$ for $s \le t-1$ .
- **Prediction Error (Innovation)**: Subtracting $\hat{y}(t|t-1)$ from the true output $y(t)$ yields the one-step-ahead prediction error $\varepsilon(t) = y(t) - \hat{y}(t|t-1) = H^{-1}(q)\left[y(t) - G(q)u(t)\right]$ , which coincides exactly with the unpredictable innovation $e(t)$ when the model matches the true system.

#### 3. Unified Synthesis of Chapter 3 Regimes
All three core operations analyzed in Chapter 3—pure simulation, $k$ -step-ahead prediction, and one-step-ahead prediction / deterministic observation—are special cases of a single two-input linear filter structure:

$$
\hat{y}(t|t-k) = W_k(q)G(q)u(t) + \left[1 - W_k(q)\right]y(t), \quad W_k(q) = \bar{H}_k(q)H^{-1}(q)
$$

| Regime | Horizon $k$ | Weighting Filter $W_k(q)$ | Input Filter on $u(t)$ | Output Filter on $y(t)$ | Physical / Operational Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **One-Step-Ahead Prediction / Observer** | $k = 1$ | $W_1(q) = H^{-1}(q)$ (since $\bar{H}_1(q) = 1$ ) | $H^{-1}(q)G(q)$ | $1 - H^{-1}(q)$ | Full correction from all past output measurements up to $t-1$ ; error equals innovation $e(t)$ . |
| **K-Step-Ahead Prediction** ( $k$ -step) | $1 < k < \infty$ | $W_k(q) = \bar{H}_k(q)H^{-1}(q)$ | $W_k(q)G(q)$ | $1 - W_k(q)$ | Uses output measurements up to $t-k$ and input plan up to $t$ ; error is the moving average $\bar{H}_k(q)e(t)$ . |
| **Pure Simulation** | $k \to \infty$ | $W_\infty(q) = 1$ (since $\bar{H}_\infty(q) = H(q)$ ) | $G(q)$ | $0$ | Open-loop response driven solely by input $u(t)$ with zero output feedback; error is full disturbance $v(t) = H(q)e(t)$ . |

## 3. Pre-Reading Compass (What to Watch Out For)

> [!warning] Key Conceptual Leaps & Notation Traps
> - **Leap 1 (From Stochastic Truth to Predictor Design Methodology)**: Do not get trapped thinking that Equation (3.46) is only valid if nature literally generates white noise $e(t)$ passed through a true transfer function $H(q)$ . Ljung explicitly emphasizes in Section 3.4 that the stochastic framework of Chapters 2 and 3 is merely a *vehicle* for specifying a predictor filter. Specifying $H(q)$ is mathematically equivalent to choosing the observer dynamics $W(q) = H^{-1}(q)$ that dictate how aggressively past output discrepancies $y(s) - G(q)u(s)$ are fed back to correct future output guesses.
> - **Leap 2 (Why Infinite-Past Predictor Formulas Need Chapter 4)**: Equation (3.46) writes the predictor in operator form using $H^{-1}(q)G(q)$ and $1 - H^{-1}(q)$ . If $H(q)$ is a rational function with a numerator polynomial $C(q)$ (for example, in ARMAX models), then $H^{-1}(q)$ has an infinite impulse response, meaning Equation (3.46) technically requires infinite data history $s \le t-1$ . Section 3.4 previews that applying Equation (3.46) to specific parameterized structures for $G$ and $H$ in Chapter 4 turns this infinite-horizon operator expression into a compact, finite-order recursive difference equation.
> - **Notation Trap (Implicit Delay in** $1 - H^{-1}(q)$ **vs.** $y(t)$ **)**: In Equation (3.46), the right-hand side writes $\left[1 - H^{-1}(q)\right]y(t)$ , which visually looks like $\hat{y}(t|t-1)$ depends on the current output $y(t)$ . Remember that because $H(q)$ is monic ( $H(q) = 1 + h(1)q^{-1} + \cdots$ ), the leading $1$ cancels out in $1 - H^{-1}(q)$ , leaving only terms in $q^{-1}, q^{-2}, \dots$ acting on $y(t)$ . Thus, only $y(t-1), y(t-2), \dots$ are actually used.
> - **Questions to Keep in Mind While Reading**:
>   1. How does changing the pole/zero locations of the noise model $H(q)$ change the relative trust placed on the deterministic input simulation $G(q)u(t)$ versus recent output measurements $y(t-1)$ in Equation (3.46)?
>   2. Why does the $k$ -step-ahead predictor naturally converge back to the open-loop simulation model $\hat{y}(t|\infty) = G(q)u(t)$ as the prediction horizon $k \to \infty$ ?
