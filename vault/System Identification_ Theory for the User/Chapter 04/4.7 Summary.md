# 4.7 Summary

> [!tip] Pre-Reading Primer
> **Role in Chapter 4**: Section 4.7 distills the entire linear time-invariant (LTI) modeling architecture of Chapter 4 into two equivalent representations—the operational predictor form and the constructive transfer-function form—contrasts the two overarching parametrization philosophies (black-box vs. physically parameterized grey-box), and summarizes how structural regularity and identifiability prepare the ground for nonlinear/time-varying extensions in Chapter 5 and parameter estimation algorithms in Part II.

---

## 1. Core Intuition & Engineering Motivation

Section 4.7 synthesizes the conceptual arc of Chapter 4 around three foundational pillars:

1. **Duality Between Operational Predictors and Constructive Model Descriptions**:
   - Every linear time-invariant model in Chapter 4 lives a double life:
     - **Operational Predictor View** (Eq. 4.147): $\hat{y}(t|\theta) = W_u(q,\theta)u(t) + W_y(q,\theta)y(t)$. This is the "operational" representation required by numerical estimation algorithms (Chapter 7), because an estimator only needs a rule that maps past observed inputs $u(t)$ and outputs $y(t)$ into a one-step-ahead prediction $\hat{y}(t|\theta)$ so it can evaluate prediction errors $\varepsilon(t,\theta) = y(t) - \hat{y}(t|\theta)$.
     - **Constructive Model Description View** (Eq. 4.148): $y(t) = G(q,\theta)u(t) + H(q,\theta)e(t)$ with white noise $\{e(t)\}$. When selecting or designing a model structure, engineers find it far more natural to reason via (4.148)—separating deterministic plant dynamics $G(q,\theta)$ from stochastic disturbance coloration $H(q,\theta)$—even though (4.147) is what actually executes during optimization.
   - These two views are in strict one-to-one correspondence via the inverse noise filter $H^{-1}(q,\theta)$.

2. **Two Competing Modeling Philosophies for Choosing $\mathcal{M}$**:
   - A parameterized model structure $\mathcal{M}$ (a family of models $\mathcal{M}(\theta)$ indexed by $\theta \in D_{\mathcal{M}} \subset \mathbf{R}^d$) provides the search space for system identification. Ljung highlights two distinct engineering philosophies for constructing $\mathcal{M}$:
     1. **Black-Box Model Structures**: Designed for maximum flexibility so that a single structural family can accommodate a wide variety of systems without requiring insight into internal physical mechanisms. The polynomial input-output families of Section 4.2 (ARX, ARMAX, OE, BJ, and the general family) and canonically parameterized discrete-time state-space structures (e.g., observer canonical form in Example 4.2 / Eq. 4.146) belong to this paradigm.
     2. **Model Structures with Physical Parameters (Grey-Box)**: Designed to embed first-principles physical insight (conservation laws, kinematics, circuit/thermal laws) directly into the model structure, reducing the adjustable parameter vector $\theta$ purely to the unknown physical constants of the plant. Continuous-time state-space models sampled to discrete time (Section 4.3) and semi-discretized distributed-parameter PDE models (Section 4.4) are the primary representatives of this approach.

3. **Formal Regularity and Identifiability as Prerequisites for Estimation**:
   - Defining a parameter vector $\theta$ is useless if the resulting predictor filters $W_u(q,\theta)$ and $W_y(q,\theta)$ are unstable/non-differentiable or if distinct parameter vectors $\theta_1 \neq \theta_2$ generate identical input-output predictors.
   - Section 4.5 introduced formal regularity and smoothness constraints on $W_u(q,\theta)$, $W_y(q,\theta)$, and $D_{\mathcal{M}}$ (Definition 4.3), while Section 4.6 and Appendix 4A investigated parameter identifiability ($\mathcal{M}(\theta_1) = \mathcal{M}(\theta_2) \Rightarrow \theta_1 = \theta_2$).
   - **The Bottom Line on Identifiability**: For standard black-box structures, global/local identifiability at $\theta^*$ can be guaranteed provided certain structural polynomial orders (or canonical observability/controllability indices) are chosen properly— avoiding pole-zero cancellations across all polynomials—and the number of such structural orders that must be fixed typically equals the number of system outputs $p$.

### 2.2 Formal Definitions, Assumptions & Step-by-Step Derivations

#### 1. The Operational Predictor Form vs. Constructive Model Form

In Section 4.7, Ljung opens by stating the two master equations that unify every linear time-invariant model structure developed in Chapter 4.

- **Operational Predictor Equation (4.147)**:

$$
\hat{y}(t|\theta) = W_u(q,\theta)u(t) + W_y(q,\theta)y(t), \qquad \theta \in D_{\mathcal{M}} \subset \mathbf{R}^d \tag{4.147}
$$

- **Constructive Transfer-Function Description (4.148)**:

$$
y(t) = G(q,\theta)u(t) + H(q,\theta)e(t), \qquad \theta \in D_{\mathcal{M}} \tag{4.148}
$$

where $\{e(t)\}$ is a sequence of independent (white noise) random variables with zero mean and covariance matrix $\Lambda(\theta)$.

#### 2. Step-by-Step Derivation of the One-to-One Correspondence

Assume $H(q,\theta)$ is monic ($H(\infty,\theta) = I$, meaning its expansion in $q^{-1}$ begins with the identity matrix $I + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} h_k(\theta)q^{-k}$) and inversely stable (all poles of $H^{-1}(z,\theta)$ lie strictly inside the unit circle $|z| < 1$ for $\theta \in D_{\mathcal{M}}$).

1. **Isolate the innovation term $e(t)$ from (4.148)**:
   Left-multiplying (4.148) by $H^{-1}(q,\theta)$ gives:

$$
H^{-1}(q,\theta)y(t) = H^{-1}(q,\theta)G(q,\theta)u(t) + e(t)
$$

2. **Decompose $H^{-1}(q,\theta)y(t)$ into current $y(t)$ and strictly past outputs**:
   Using the identity $y(t) = [I - H^{-1}(q,\theta)]y(t) + H^{-1}(q,\theta)y(t)$, substitute $H^{-1}(q,\theta)y(t)$:

$$
y(t) = H^{-1}(q,\theta)G(q,\theta)u(t) + \left[I - H^{-1}(q,\theta)\right]y(t) + e(t)
$$

3. **Take the conditional expectation given past data $Z^{t-1} = \{u(t-1), y(t-1), u(t-2), y(t-2), \dots\}$**:
   Because $H(q,\theta)$ is monic, $I - H^{-1}(q,\theta)$ has zero direct feedthrough (it contains only strictly positive powers of $q^{-1}$, i.e., $q^{-1}, q^{-2}, \dots$), so $[I - H^{-1}(q,\theta)]y(t)$ depends exclusively on past outputs $y(s)$ for $s \le t-1$. Meanwhile, $e(t)$ is white noise independent of past data, so its conditional expectation is zero. Defining the one-step-ahead predictor $\hat{y}(t|\theta)$ by dropping $e(t)$ yields the exact mapping on page 119:

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_u(q,\theta) &= H^{-1}(q,\theta)G(q,\theta) \\
W_y(q,\theta) &= \left[1 - H^{-1}(q,\theta)\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

*(Note: In SISO scalar notation, the identity operator is written as $1$; in MIMO systems of dimension $p$, it represents the identity matrix $I_p$.)*

Conversely, given any operational predictor filters $W_u(q,\theta)$ and $W_y(q,\theta)$ where $1 - W_y(q,\theta)$ is monic and invertible, we uniquely recover the constructive model via:

$$
H(q,\theta) = \left[1 - W_y(q,\theta)\right]^{-1}, \qquad G(q,\theta) = \left[1 - W_y(q,\theta)\right]^{-1}W_u(q,\theta)
$$

> [!tip] Why Do We Need Both Equations (4.147) and (4.148)?
> - **Designers think in (4.148)**: Physical intuition, Bode plots, pole-zero locations, delays $n_k$, and disturbance spectra $\Phi_v(\omega) = \lambda |H(e^{i\omega},\theta)|^2$ are transparent in $G(q,\theta)$ and $H(q,\theta)$.
> - **Algorithms compute in (4.147)**: Parameter estimation algorithms (Part II) evaluate the prediction error $\varepsilon(t,\theta) = y(t) - \hat{y}(t|\theta) = [1 - W_y(q,\theta)]y(t) - W_u(q,\theta)u(t)$ and its parameter gradient $\psi(t,\theta) = \frac{d}{d\theta}\hat{y}(t|\theta)$ directly through the predictor filters $W_u(q,\theta)$ and $W_y(q,\theta)$.

#### 3. Master Synthesis Table of Chapter 4 Model Structures

The table below synthesizes all major model structures studied across Chapter 4 under the unified lens of Section 4.7:

| Model Structure | Constructive Filters $G(q,\theta)$ and $H(q,\theta)$ | Operational Predictor Filters $W_u(q,\theta)$ and $W_y(q,\theta)$ | Linearity in $\theta$ ($\hat{y}(t|\theta)$) | Philosophy & Identifiability Condition (Sec. 4.6) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FIR** (Finite Impulse Response) | $G = B(q)$, $H = 1$ | $W_u = B(q)$, $W_y = 0$ | **Linear** ($\varphi^T(t)\theta$) | Black-box; always globally identifiable ($\det R > 0$). |
| **ARX** (Equation Error) | $G = \frac{B(q)}{A(q)}$, $H = \frac{1}{A(q)}$ | $W_u = B(q)$, $W_y = 1 - A(q)$ | **Linear** ($\varphi^T(t)\theta$) | Black-box; globally identifiable iff $A(q)$ and $B(q)$ are coprime (no common factors) and delay $n_k$ is exact. |
| **ARMAX** | $G = \frac{B(q)}{A(q)}$, $H = \frac{C(q)}{A(q)}$ | $W_u = \frac{B(q)}{C(q)}$, $W_y = 1 - \frac{A(q)}{C(q)}$ | **Nonlinear** (Pseudolinear) | Black-box; identifiable iff $\gcd(A,B,C) = 1$ (no factor common to all three) and $C(z)$ is monic/stable. |
| **OE** (Output Error) | $G = \frac{B(q)}{F(q)}$, $H = 1$ | $W_u = \frac{B(q)}{F(q)}$, $W_y = 0$ | **Nonlinear** (Output recursion) | Black-box; identifiable iff $B(q)$ and $F(q)$ are coprime ($B(z^*) \not\equiv 0$). |
| **BJ** (Box-Jenkins) | $G = \frac{B(q)}{F(q)}$, $H = \frac{C(q)}{D(q)}$ | $W_u = \frac{D(q)B(q)}{C(q)F(q)}$, $W_y = 1 - \frac{D(q)}{C(q)}$ | **Nonlinear** | Black-box; identifiable iff $(B,F)$ are coprime and $(C,D)$ are coprime independently. |
| **General Input-Output** (4.33) | $G = \frac{B(q)}{A(q)F(q)}$, $H = \frac{C(q)}{A(q)D(q)}$ | $W_u = \frac{D(q)B(q)}{C(q)F(q)}$, $W_y = 1 - \frac{A(q)D(q)}{C(q)}$ | **Nonlinear** | Black-box; requires simultaneous coprimeness across shared $(A,B,C)$ and unshared $(B,F), (C,D)$ pairs (Theorem 4.1). |
| **Canonical State-Space** (e.g., Eq. 4.146) | $G = C(qI - A)^{-1}B$, $H = I + C(qI - A)^{-1}K$ | $W_u = C(qI - A + KC)^{-1}B$, $W_y = C(qI - A + KC)^{-1}K$ | **Nonlinear** | Black-box ($3n$ parameters in SISO observer form); identifiable iff $\{A(\theta^*), [B(\theta^*)\ \ K(\theta^*)]\}$ is controllable (and observable by construction). |
| **Physically Parameterized State-Space** (Sec. 4.3) | $G, H$ derived by sampling $\dot{x} = \mathcal{A}(\theta)x + \mathcal{B}(\theta)u + v$ | State Kalman/innovations filter | **Nonlinear** (via matrix exponentials) | **Physical / Grey-box**; identifiability depends on whether distinct physical constants $\theta$ map injectively to Markov parameters / canonical coefficients. |

## 3. Pre-Reading Compass (What to Watch Out For)

> [!warning] Key Conceptual Leaps & Notation Traps
> - **Leap 1 — Why "Choosing Order Properly" Equals the Number of Outputs $p$**: At the very end of Section 4.7 (page 119), Ljung remarks: *"The bottom line of these results is that identifiability can be secured, provided certain orders are chosen properly. The number of such orders to be chosen typically equals the number of outputs."* In SISO systems ($p=1$), this refers to fixing the single overall McMillan degree / canonical order $n$ (or fixing the maximal polynomial degree so that $A, B, C$ have no common factors, i.e., minimal order $n$). In MIMO systems ($p > 1$, detailed in Appendix 4A), full polynomial or state-space canonical forms require specifying $p$ observability/Kronecker indices $n_1, n_2, \dots, n_p$ (summing to the state dimension $n = \sum_{i=1}^p n_i$), one for each output channel, to uniquely pin down a canonical parametrization.
> - **Leap 2 — Canonical State-Space is Classified as "Black-Box"**: Notice that under bullet 1 on page 119, Ljung explicitly groups *canonically parameterized state-space models* (like the observer canonical form in Example 4.2 and Eq. 4.146) under **black-box model structures**, NOT physical models. Even though they use state-space matrices $(A, B, C, K)$, their free parameters (" $\times$" entries in Eq. 4.146b) are merely canonical polynomial coefficients chosen for representational flexibility, devoid of direct physical meaning. True physical state-space models belong to bullet 2 (Section 4.3).
> - **Notation Trap — Scalar `1` vs. Matrix `I` in Predictor Filter Formulas**: In $W_y(q,\theta) = [1 - H^{-1}(q,\theta)]$, the `1` represents the identity operator (the identity matrix $I_p$ when $y(t) \in \mathbf{R}^p$ is a vector). Also remember that because $H(q,\theta)$ is monic ($H(\infty,\theta) = I$), $H^{-1}(q,\theta)$ is also monic ($H^{-1}(\infty,\theta) = I$), which guarantees that $1 - H^{-1}(q,\theta)$ has **no** $q^0$ term—ensuring $\hat{y}(t|\theta)$ never uses the current unmeasured $y(t)$ to predict $y(t)$.
> - **Questions to Keep in Mind While Reading**:
>   1. When would an engineer prefer a black-box structure (like ARMAX or canonical state-space) over a physical continuous-time grey-box structure, and what is the trade-off in parameter dimension $d = \dim \theta$ vs. physical interpretability?
>   2. How does the operational predictor equation $\hat{y}(t|\theta) = W_u(q,\theta)u(t) + W_y(q,\theta)y(t)$ generalize when the underlying system is nonlinear or time-varying (the immediate subject of Chapter 5)?
